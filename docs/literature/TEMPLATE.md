## 1. What problem does it attack?

One paragraph, in your own words. Not the abstract reworded.

## 2. What is the method?

The core idea in three or four sentences. If it is a front-end, say what goes
in and what comes out. If it is a model, say the shape.

## 3. What data did they use?

Dataset and partition. If it is not ASVspoof 2019 LA, say so clearly — the
number in section 4 is then not comparable with ours.

## 4. What did they report?

| Metric | Value | On which partition |
|---|---|---|
| EER % |  |  |
| min t-DCF |  |  |

**Copy these numbers from the paper. If the paper does not state one, write
"not reported" — do not calculate, estimate or guess it.** These figures end up
in our final report's related-work table, and a wrong one there is worse than a
blank.

## 5. What do we take from it?

What concretely lands in our code, and in which phase or experiment. "Nothing"
is a valid and common answer.

## 6. What do we deliberately not take, and why?

Usually: out of scope per CLAUDE.md, too expensive to train, or needs data we
do not have.

## 7. One thing you did not understand

Be honest. This is what we discuss as a group. An empty section here usually
means the paper was skimmed, not read.
